# Pending glossary review

Terms tagged inline with `{CODE}` during episode translation, staged here for
a periodic review pass (not resolved per-episode). Once a term is confirmed
against a reliable source (or fixed as this project's own convention), add it
to `naruto_glosario_es_es.yml` and strip the `{CODE}` tag from every episode
that used it.

Append new entries at the end — do not edit existing ones — so concurrent
episode translations don't conflict on this file.

Entry format (parsed by `scripts/build_site_data.py` to show a tooltip for
the tag in the site's episode viewer):

```
### `{CODE}` - short title
Free-text explanation of the term/decision, as long as needed.
```

### `{INOCERDA}` - Ino Pig (apodo que Sakura da a Ino)
Sakura llama a Ino «Ino Pig» (S01E06, cue 591). Se ha traducido como «Ino-cerda», con el mismo esquema que el apodo de Sakura («Pista de Aterrizaje»). Pendiente de contrastar con el doblaje de España. Comprobado 2026-09-24 contra la base OCR del manga/guía: sin coincidencia.

### `{PAWENC}` - Paw Encyclopedia (S01E05)
English "Paw Encyclopedia", the book of cat paw prints Sasuke and Itachi compiled. Translated literally as "Enciclopedia de Patas". The official Spain rendering is unknown. Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: no hit — likely anime-filler content not covered by the manga.

### `{MATATABI}` - silvervine drink (S01E05)
English "silvervine drink" (the plant cats are drawn to, matatabi in Japanese). Kept as "bebida de matatabi" to preserve the cat-plant reference. Alternatives: "actinidia" or a plain "bebida para gatos". Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: no hit.

### `{VENDETTA}` - vendetta (S01E05)
English "vendetta" / "vendetta permit" / "34 vendettas" for the legal revenge-killing arc. Kept as "vendetta" (common in Spain Spanish) with "permiso de vendetta". Alternative: "venganza" / "permiso de venganza". Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: no hit (only unrelated "venganza" hits about Sasuke's arc) — this is an anime-filler arc, not in the manga.

### `{LOCK}` - Lock Village and Land of Key (S01E05)
English "Hidden Lock Village" in the "Land of Key", later shortened to "Lock Village" and "ninja of the Lock". Rendered "Villa Oculta del Cerrojo", "País de la Llave", "Villa del Cerrojo". The official village and country names are unverified (follows the "Villa Oculta de la Hoja" convention). Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: no hit — anime-filler location, not in the manga.

### `{PACHINKO}` - Pachinko Jutsu (S01E05)
Combination attack named "Pachinko Jutsu" in the English. Rendered "Técnica Pachinko". Unknown whether the Spanish edition transliterates or adapts it. Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: one hit (Naruto vol.18 p.59) but it's a footnote about a literal pachinko gambling machine, unrelated to this filler combo attack.

### `{PESCA}` - Shadow Clone Jutsu: Fishing Version
Naruto's joke technique rendered "Técnica de Clones de Sombra: Versión Pesca", introduced by "Técnica especial:" (S01E07, cue 1010). Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: no hit — anime-filler joke technique.

### `{KIBA}` - Kiba's Man Beast Taijutsu Technique (remaining)
S01E08: "Man Beast Taijutsu Technique" rendered "Técnica de Taijutsu Hombre-Bestia" (cue 932). "Técnica animal", "Técnica de las Cuatro Garras" and "Multiplicación Bestial" are already resolved in the glossary; "Fang Over Fang" was resolved 2026-09-24 as "Colmillos Cruzados" (guía oficial 1 p.174). This specific one is still unconfirmed: the guide's p.196 entry "¡¡La combinación hombre-bestia!!" is tagged as Ninjutsu, not Taijutsu, and doesn't match the "Técnica de Taijutsu..." wording exactly, so it's not a confident enough match to resolve.

### `{SANGRE}` - Hot-Blooded (young Lee's Academy nickname)
In the Academy-days flashback, Lee is mocked as "Hot-Blooded" / "Hot-Blooded loser" (S01E08, cues 1485-1490). Rendered "Sangre Caliente" / "Perdedor de Sangre Caliente". No source found for an official rendering; pending confirmation. Checked 2026-09-24 against the manga/guide OCR database: no hit — likely anime-only flashback content.

### `{CLOSET}` - Closet Perv (Naruto's nickname for Ebisu)
Naruto calls Ebisu "The Closet Perv" / "Closet pervert" on first meeting him (S01E09, first use cue 233). Rendered "el Pervertido de Armario", a literal calque of the English "closet" (hidden/secret) metaphor. No official Spanish-dub or manga source found; the character guide only confirms "pervertido" as a listed personality trait for Ebisu, not this specific nickname. Only the first occurrence is tagged. Re-checked 2026-09-24: still no hit for a "closet"/"armario" nickname specifically.

### `{MYOBOKU}` - Great Toad Sage of Mount Myoboku
Jiraiya's mock self-introduction the first time Naruto meets him, "I am the Great Toad Sage of Mount Myoboku, the immortal... also known as the Toad Mountain Sage" (S01E09, cue 447-448). Rendered "el Sabio Sapo del Monte Myoboku" / "el Sabio Montaña Sapo". "Monte Myoboku" itself is confirmed by the Planeta character guide (recurring as "monte Myóboku"); the "Great Toad Sage" title is Jiraiya's own comedic exaggeration in-universe (not the same as "Ogama Sennin", the actual toad elder character named later in the guide) and has no direct official rendering. The place name is confirmed; only the title "Sabio Sapo" is pending. Re-checked 2026-09-24: guide vol.3 p.84 confirms "Ogama Sennin" as the distinct real toad-elder name, matching this entry's own reasoning; still no hit for a "Gran Sabio Sapo" title itself.

### `{TRIGRAMAS}` - Eight Trigrams Sealing Style / Four-Symbols Seal
Jiraiya's internal analysis of Naruto's seal: "Two Four-Symbols Seals making up an Eight Trigrams Seal... So it's an Eight Trigrams Sealing Style" (S01E09, cue 602-603). Rendered "Sello de los Cuatro Símbolos" and "Sello/Estilo de Sellado de los Ocho Trigramas". No source found in the manga/guide database; literal translation of the English fansub terms (the seal's canonical Japanese name is Hakke no Fūin Shiki). Checked 2026-09-24: no hit for "trigrama(s)" anywhere in the OCR database — Planeta consistently keeps "hakke" as an untranslated loanword instead (e.g. "Hakke rokujúyon shó", "Hakke kú shó"; see the now-resolved 64PALMAS = "64 signos hakke"). Suggests "Ocho Trigramas" may not be how Spain renders "hakke" here either, but no direct hit for this specific seal concept (distinct from Neji's techniques), so left pending rather than guessed.

### `{DANZALUNA}` - Leaf Style: Crescent Moon Dance
A sword technique used against Zaku during the forest sequence, "Leaf Style: Crescent Moon Dance" (S01E09, cue 742-743). Rendered "Estilo Hoja: Danza de la Luna Creciente". No source found; literal translation, unverified against manga/guide. Checked 2026-09-24: only a coincidental hit, "Tajo de Luna Creciente de la Escuela de la Nube" (guide vol.3 p.318) — a different technique/style (Cloud "tajo"/slash, not Leaf "danza"/dance), not the same move.

### `{CHUNIN}` - Chūnin/Jōnin macron inconsistency
The glossary's verified:false entry for "Chūnin"/"Jōnin" keeps the macron, and S01E01/S01E03 used it that way, but S01E09 (Christian-reviewed, 2026-09-22) dropped the macron to "Chunin"/"Jonin" throughout. S01E10 continues directly from S01E09's arc, so it follows S01E09's no-macron spelling for consistency with the immediately preceding episode, diverging from the glossary and from S01E01/S01E03. Only the first occurrence in S01E10 is tagged (cue 102); the word is too frequent to tag every instance. Needs a decision on which spelling is canonical, then a pass to unify S01E01/S01E03 (or S01E09/S01E10) to match. Checked 2026-09-24: Planeta doesn't use "Chunin"/"Jonin" as loanwords at all — it translates the rank as "grado medio" throughout (e.g. Naruto vol.4 pp.140-164). Interesting, but doesn't settle the macron question on its own since the loanword itself isn't used in the source.

### `{HOZVIENTO}` - Ninja Art: Wind Scythe Jutsu
Temari's fan technique, "Ninja Art: Wind Scythe Jutsu!" (S01E10, cue 658). Rendered "Arte ninja: Técnica de la Hoz de Viento!", using the already-resolved "Arte ninja:" prefix. No official Spain source found for "Wind Scythe" itself; "Hoz de Viento" is a literal translation, unverified against manga/guide. Checked 2026-09-24: guide vol.2 p.210 describes Temari's ultimate wind-scythe technique, "Danza Cortante" (weasel-summoning, "una guadaña que lo corta todo"), and the technique index lists other Temari wind/scythe entries ("Filo del viento", "Espirales guadaña", "Grandes espirales guadaña") — several candidates exist but none titled "Hoz de Viento" exactly, and none clearly matches this specific simple S01E10 shout. Left pending rather than guessing which one (if any) applies.
